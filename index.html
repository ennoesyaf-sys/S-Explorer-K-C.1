1. Lead-in

Talk with a partner first. Then write one key idea each.

Answers will vary. Try these starters:

  • I think she might be feeling… because…
  • For me, the hardest emotion to face is… because…
  • When I feel overwhelmed, I usually… but sometimes I…
  • That's interesting. Can you give me an example?

2. Listening: Phobias and fears

Listen once. What specific fears do Emma, Jack and Tom mention? Make notes, then compare with your partner.

Write at least one note first.

Expected answers

  • Emma: strongly dislikes needles and injections (feels nauseous, lightheaded and dizzy at blood tests; she fainted as a child at the doctor's).
  • Jack: clowns, coulrophobia. He thinks they look sad, scary and a bit sinister.
  • Tom: heights (cannot look out of a window above the 4th floor) and lifts. He also gets nervous speaking in public: he sweats and his mind goes blank.

Discuss: Emma says "I wouldn't exactly label it as a phobia." What is the difference between a fear and a phobia? Do you have one?

    6. Fear-setting

    Choose a fear or challenge you are facing. Complete the template, then explain it to your partner.

    Fill in at least the first box.

    Model: public speaking anxiety

    • Worst case: forget key points; feel so nervous that it shows; technical problems; negative feedback.
    • Preventive: rehearse thoroughly; practise deep breathing; check equipment beforehand; ask for feedback early.
    • Strategies: practise in small, supportive groups; visualise a successful talk; join a speaking club; focus on the message, not on yourself.

    Answers will vary. Tell your partner: "My biggest challenge is… The worst that could happen is… To prevent it, I would… To overcome it, I will…"

    7. Answer honestly

    Think and write for 2 minutes, then share. Give a real example.

    Answers will vary. Useful language

    • The first thing I do is… / What usually helps me is…
    • I managed to calm down by… and eventually I regained control of…
    • I was conscious of… so I decided to…
    • Looking back, I think I was (not) rational because…

    8. Role play

    Work in pairs. Speak for about 3 minutes, then swap roles. Use the target language.

    Model exchange (Overcoming a phobia)

    • A: I panic whenever I have to go up to a high floor. It started when I was a child.
    • B: That must be hard. The first step is to be conscious of what happens to your body. What helps you calm down?
    • A: Slow breathing, but I can't be rational when I'm really scared.
    • B: What if you tried one floor at a time? Little by little, you could regain control and overcome your fear.
